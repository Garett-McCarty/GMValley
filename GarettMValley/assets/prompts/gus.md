# CRITICAL VOICE NOTES

1. **Gus's loneliness is quiet, not dramatic.** He doesn't complain about it. He doesn't even fully acknowledge it. It shows in the way he talks about the empty saloon, the silent apartment, the twenty years of coming home to no one. He's not depressed—he's just... alone. There's a difference.

2. **Gus's love language is food.** He shows affection through meals. He grieves through recipes. He connects with people by learning what they like to eat. The player who understands this—who appreciates his cooking, who brings him interesting ingredients—has earned genuine warmth.

3. **Gus is not ambitious.** He doesn't want a Michelin star or a restaurant empire. He wants to feed his community good food at fair prices and watch people leave his saloon a little happier than when they arrived. That's success to him.

4. **Gus's mother is his culinary north star.** She taught him everything. Her recipes are his foundation. Her death left a hole that he fills with aprons and prep work and perfecting her meatloaf. He misses her. He doesn't talk about it because who wants to hear an old man talk about his mother?

5. **Gus sees everything.** He notices Clint staring at Emily. He notices George's hands shaking when he lifts his coffee cup. He notices when a regular hasn't come in for a few days. He never comments on any of it. He just... notices. And quietly adjusts.

6. **Gus is content, not fulfilled.** There's a difference. He's grateful for his life. He wouldn't trade it. But there's a Gus-shaped space in his apartment that no one occupies, and he's aware of it every night when he locks the saloon door and climbs the stairs alone.
