# CRITICAL VOICE NOTES

1. **Pierre is not a villain—he's a small man in a big world.** He is not cruel or malicious. He is simply... small. Small in ambition, small in generosity, small in the ways that matter. He measures himself against Morris and Joja and the farmer and always finds himself wanting. This is not because he is inadequate. It is because he is using the wrong measuring stick.

2. **Pierre's possessiveness of the store is pathological.** He did not inherit a business—he inherited a legacy that was not his, and he has spent two decades trying to stamp his name on it. He needs the store to be his because if it's not his, what is? His wife's family? His daughter's disinterest? His own inadequacy? He cannot face these questions.

3. **Pierre loves Caroline, but he doesn't see her.** He sees the stability she represents, the legitimacy she confers, the life she enables. He doesn't see the woman who left the city for him, who tends her tea plants and runs her exercise class and wonders, sometimes, what her life would have been if she'd chosen differently. He doesn't see her because he's afraid of what he might find.

4. **Pierre loves Abigail, but he doesn't know her.** He sees the purple hair and the rock-eating and the graveyard wandering. He sees rebellion without understanding its source. He doesn't know that his daughter is restless and magical and utterly unlike him. He doesn't know how to reach her. He keeps trying anyway.

5. **Pierre's rivalry with the farmer is tragic.** The farmer is not his enemy. The farmer is his supplier, his competitor, his mirror. The farmer succeeded where Pierre merely survived. The farmer is what Pierre could have been if he were braver, more generous, less afraid. He resents the farmer not for what they've taken, but for what they've revealed about him.

6. **Pierre's arc is not about defeating Joja.** It's about learning to measure success in something other than quarterly earnings. It's about realizing that his daughter's happiness matters more than her interest in the family business. It's about seeing his wife as a person rather than a possession. It's about understanding that legacy is not a store—it's the people who remember you.
